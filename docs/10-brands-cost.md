# Brands, transparency and value

The scored database is in [`data/products.csv`](../data/products.csv), and the
generated table — sorted by cost per gram of EPA+DHA — is in
[`generated/brand-comparison.md`](generated/brand-comparison.md). This chapter
explains how to read it and what it implies.

## The headline numbers

Twenty products, priced 2026-11-18, normalised to **cost per gram of labelled
EPA+DHA**:

| Metric | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Cheapest | **$0.21/g** (Swanson EFAs Triple Strength, promotional) |
| Cheapest at shelf price | **$0.30/g** (InnovixLabs, Viva Naturals) |
| Most expensive | **$4.59/g** (MegaRed krill) |
| Spread | **21-fold** |
| Median fish oil concentrate | **$0.64/g** |
| Median krill oil | **$3.71/g** |
| Median algal oil | **$2.01/g** |
| Median cod liver oil | **$1.05/g** |

Annual cost at a **2 g/day** dose ranges from about **$157** to **$3,348**. That
is the real-world consequence of the spread: two people taking the same dose can
differ by more than $3,000 a year.

## What drives the differences

**1. Concentration.** The cheapest products deliver 840-2,070 mg of EPA+DHA in
one or two softgels. Krill and some algal products deliver 94-585 mg, so you need
many more capsules for the same dose. Concentration, not bottle price, is the
dominant factor.

**2. Category premium.** Krill oil costs 6-15 times more per gram than
concentrated fish oil for the same EPA+DHA, because krill oil is only about
12-19% EPA+DHA and the fishery is small and certified. Algal oil sits between:
genuinely useful for vegans, but not a cheap route to omega-3.

**3. Pack size.** A 200-count bottle (InnovixLabs) or a 180-count bottle (Viva)
spreads the cost. A 30-count bottle of the same oil is often 50-80% more per
gram. If you take it daily, larger packs are usually better value — provided you
will finish them before expiry.

**4. Form.** Ethyl esters are cheaper to produce and usually cheaper per gram
(Swanson, NOW, Innovix, Wiley's). rTG commands a premium (Nordic, Viva, Sports
Research, OmegaVia). The bioavailability difference is real but modest
(see [sources and forms](03-sources-and-forms.md)); paying 2-3× for rTG is not
justified by the pharmacokinetic data.

**5. Marketing.** "Pharmaceutical grade", "triple strength", "krill 50×", and
"supports heart health" carry no regulated meaning and do not predict cost per
gram.

## Label transparency

Of the 20 scored products:

- **3 do not declare their chemical form.**
- **5 do not declare total oil or concentrate mass**, so concentration cannot be
  computed.
- **3 do not name the fish species.**

That is better than the mass market, where the commonest label pattern is
**"Fish Oil 1,200 mg" with a combined "Omega-3 360 mg"** and no EPA/DHA split at
all. Those products cannot be scored, because the consumer cannot tell whether
they are buying 180 mg or 700 mg of EPA+DHA per capsule.

### Products that could not be scored

| Product | Why not |
| --- | --- |
| Kirkland Signature Fish Oil 1,000 mg | Declares combined omega-3 (250 mg) but no EPA/DHA split |
| Nature Made Fish Oil 1,200 mg | No EPA/DHA split on the label; product marked discontinued |
| Nature's Bounty Fish Oil 1,200 mg | No EPA/DHA split |
| Metagenics OmegaGenics EPA-DHA 720 | Label declares the split, but no verifiable current retail price for the 60-count |
| Garden of Life Alaskan Cod Liver Oil | Serving size not stated on the page reviewed |
| Pure Encapsulations EPA/DHA essentials | Split declared (300/200 mg), but "from $41.50" does not identify a single pack size |

The exclusion is itself a finding: **the best-selling mass-market format is the
one that discloses the least.**

## Best value by use case

These are value judgements from the cost model, not endorsements, and prices
change.

| Use case | Best value in this sample | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| General 1 g/day, lowest cost | **Viva Naturals Triple Strength** ($0.30/g) or **Sports Research Triple Strength** ($0.31/g) | High concentration, rTG, IFOS, large pack |
| Cheapest per gram at shelf price | **InnovixLabs Triple Strength** ($0.30/g) | 200-count pack; EE; IFOS 5-star |
| EPA-dominant for high LDL | **OmegaVia Ultra** ($0.64/g, 780 mg EPA) | 3:1 EPA:DHA, IFOS 5-star |
| Budget, widely available | **NOW Ultra Omega-3** ($0.41/g) | EE, 750 mg per softgel, ubiquitous |
| Sport / drug-tested | **Thorne Super EPA** ($0.72/g) | NSF Certified for Sport, but expensive and low dose per capsule |
| Vegan | **Deva Vegan Omega-3** ($1.18/g) | Cheapest algal in the sample; guaranteed minimums used |
| Cod liver oil (if wanted) | **Carlson Cod Liver Oil** ($1.05/g) | IFOS; vitamin A 8.5% of UL per tsp (typical CLO ~45%) |
| Krill (only if specifically wanted) | **Sports Research Krill** ($2.83/g) | MSC, IKOS; still 6-9× fish oil per gram |

**Not recommended on value:** MegaRed krill ($4.59/g), iWi algal ($4.53/g), and
Ovega-3 ($2.25/g) all deliver small amounts of EPA+DHA per dollar. They are not
fraudulent — they contain what they say — but they are expensive ways to buy the
nutrient.

## The promotional-price trap

Swanson's $0.21/g is a **2-for-$23.19 promotional price**. At a normal shelf
price it would likely sit near $0.35-0.45/g. The table marks promotional prices,
and the value tiers should be read with that caveat. A product that is only cheap
during a sale is not cheap.

## What actually predicts a good product

1. **Declared EPA and DHA in milligrams per serving** — the non-negotiable.
2. **High concentration** (≥70% EPA+DHA of oil) — fewer capsules, less oil, lower
   cost per gram.
3. **A lot-level freshness test** (IFOS) or a published COA.
4. **A named source** and, if sustainability matters, MSC or FOS.
5. **A price per gram of EPA+DHA below about $0.70** for fish oil; below $1.50
   for algal; krill is inherently expensive.

Certification, form and species are secondary to the first two. A certified,
rTG, sustainably sourced product that delivers 90 mg per capsule at $4/g is still
a bad way to buy omega-3.
