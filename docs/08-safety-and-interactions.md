# Safety, side effects and interactions

## The short version

At doses up to about 3 g/day, omega-3 supplements are well tolerated by most
people. The clinically relevant issues are **atrial fibrillation at higher
doses**, **gastrointestinal effects**, a **small LDL-C rise with DHA**, and
**vitamin A toxicity with cod liver oil**. Bleeding risk is real but much smaller
than commonly feared.

## Bleeding

The fear is plausible: omega-3 displaces arachidonic acid from platelet
membranes and modestly reduces platelet aggregation. The evidence does not
support a clinically important bleeding risk at usual doses.

| Evidence | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| Meta-analysis, 11 RCTs, 120,643 patients | Bleeding RR **1.09 (0.91-1.31)** — not significant ([R49](references.md)) |
| High-dose purified EPA subgroup | ~50% relative increase, **0.6% absolute** |
| OPERA, 1,516 cardiac surgery patients, 8-10 g/day pre-op | Major bleeding OR **0.81 (0.53-1.24)**; **fewer transfusions** ([R52](references.md)) |
| Cochrane bleeding outcome | RR 1.12 (0.91-1.37), very low certainty ([R05](references.md)) |
| Review of 19 trials up to 21 g/day | Clinically significant bleeding "virtually non-existent" ([R50](references.md)) |

**Perioperative guidance:** the evidence argues *against* routinely stopping
omega-3 before surgery ([R51](references.md), [R52](references.md)). No major
society guideline mandates a 7-day stop. Patients on multiple antithrombotic
drugs, or with a bleeding disorder, should still individualise the decision with
their clinician.

## Atrial fibrillation

This is the most important safety signal in the field, and it is dose-dependent:

| Trial / analysis | Dose | AF result |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Gencer meta-analysis (7 RCTs, 81,210) | 0.84-4 g/day | HR **1.25 (1.07-1.46)**; **1.49** above 1 g/day |
| STRENGTH | 4 g/day EPA+DHA | **2.2% vs 1.3%**, HR 1.69 (1.29-2.21), NNH 114 |
| REDUCE-IT | 4 g/day icosapent ethyl | **5.3% vs 3.9%** |
| OMEMI | 1.7 g/day post-MI | HR 1.90 (1.16-3.11) |

The EMA added a dose-dependent AF warning to omega-3-acid ethyl ester medicines
in 2023, noting the highest risk at 4 g/day in patients with established CVD
([R55](references.md)).

**What this means practically:** at 1 g/day the absolute AF risk is small. At 3-4
g/day it is material, and for someone with paroxysmal AF or a high risk of AF,
the balance may favour not escalating the dose. It does not mean low-dose omega-3
causes AF in healthy people.

## Gastrointestinal effects

The most common reason people stop taking fish oil.

| Effect | Estimate |
| --- | --- |
| Diarrhoea | OR **1.245 (1.048-1.480)** vs placebo ([R60](references.md)) |
| Taste disturbance / fishy taste | OR **3.478 (1.789-6.762)** ([R60](references.md)) |
| Eructation (prescription omega-3-acid ethyl esters) | 4% vs 1% ([R116](references.md)) |
| GI adverse events, omega-3 carboxylic acids | 24.7% vs 14.7% in STRENGTH ([R03](references.md)) |

Mitigations that work: take with food, split the dose, freeze capsules briefly
before swallowing, choose a concentrated product (fewer capsules for the same
dose), or use enteric-coated capsules. Enteric coating reduces burping but does
not improve absorption.

## LDL cholesterol and DHA

DHA raises LDL-C; EPA does not.

- DHA vs placebo: **+7.23 mg/dL** (3.98-10.5) ([R61](references.md)).
- DHA vs EPA head-to-head: **+4.63 mg/dL** (2.15-7.10) ([R61](references.md)).
- ComparED, 2.7 g/day: DHA raised LDL **3.3%** more than EPA
  ([R62](references.md)).
- EFSA: EPA up to 4 g/day has no significant LDL effect; combined EPA+DHA at 2-6
  g/day raises LDL about 3% ([R45](references.md)).

For most people this is clinically trivial. For someone with familial
hypercholesterolaemia or poorly controlled LDL, it argues for an EPA-dominant
product.

## Vitamin A and D toxicity

This is the specific hazard of **cod liver oil**, and it is under-appreciated.

- Adult upper limit for preformed vitamin A: **3,000 mcg RAE/day**
  ([R57](references.md), [R59](references.md)).
- A leading cod liver oil supplies **255 mcg RAE per teaspoon — 85% of the UL** —
  before any other dietary vitamin A ([R57](references.md)).
- "High-vitamin" cod liver oils can supply several times more.
- Documented cases of hypervitaminosis A from cod liver oil exist, including
  hypercalcaemia and serum retinol 220 µg/dL after 1-2 months of high-dose tablets
  ([R56](references.md)).
- Vitamin A is **teratogenic**; cod liver oil is a poor choice in pregnancy for
  this reason.
- Vitamin D upper limit: **4,000 IU/day (100 mcg)** for adults
  ([R58](references.md)).

If you take cod liver oil plus a multivitamin plus a separate vitamin D
supplement, you can exceed both limits without noticing. Cod liver oil should be
counted as a vitamin product.

## Medication interactions

| Drug / class | Interaction | Practical advice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Warfarin | Case reports of INR elevation; controlled trial at 3-6 g/day found no significant change ([R53](references.md), [R54](references.md)) | Monitor INR when starting or changing dose; not contraindicated |
| DOACs / antiplatelets | Additive theoretical bleeding risk; no large trial signal | No routine dose adjustment; caution with multiple agents |
| Statins | Additive triglyceride lowering; no adverse interaction of note | Commonly combined deliberately |
| Orlistat | Reduces fat absorption, including omega-3 | Separate dosing or accept reduced absorption |
| Antihypertensives | Small additive BP reduction | Monitor if on multiple agents |
| Bile acid sequestrants | May reduce omega-3 absorption | Separate dosing |

A systematic review rated the warfarin evidence "inconclusive, with two studies
reporting increased effect and one no effect" ([R53](references.md)). This is a
monitor-and-continue situation, not a contraindication.

## Long-term and high-dose safety

- Trials have run for **5-7.4 years** at 1 g/day (VITAL, ASCEND) with no excess
  of serious adverse events or bleeding.
- At **4 g/day**, the main long-term signal is atrial fibrillation.
- At **>5 g/day**, the FDA has said the evidence is insufficient to conclude
  safety, and EFSA could not set a tolerable upper intake level.
- There is no evidence that long-term omega-3 supplementation causes cancer,
  kidney disease, or liver injury at usual doses.

## Who should be cautious

| Group | Concern |
| --- | --- |
| Prior atrial fibrillation or high AF risk | Dose-dependent AF signal above 1 g/day |
| On warfarin or multiple antithrombotics | Bleeding/INR monitoring |
| High LDL-C or familial hypercholesterolaemia | DHA raises LDL; prefer EPA |
| Pregnant | Avoid cod liver oil (vitamin A); discuss DHA dose |
| Infants and young children | No established supplemental dose; use formula or clinician advice |
| Fish or shellfish allergy | Fish oil and gelatin shells may be unsuitable; algal oil is an alternative |
| Liver disease or vitamin A excess | Avoid cod liver oil |
| Upcoming surgery | Evidence does not require stopping; individualise with the surgeon |
| Pancreatitis with severe hypertriglyceridaemia | Needs medical management, not self-dosing |
