# Evidence review

The full graded dataset is in
[`generated/evidence-tables.md`](generated/evidence-tables.md) (70 rows). This
chapter explains what the numbers mean and where the marketing departs from them.

## Cardiovascular disease

### Primary prevention: mostly null, with one real signal

**VITAL** randomised 25,871 US adults aged 50+ without CVD to 1 g/day of marine
omega-3 (460 mg EPA + 380 mg DHA) or olive oil, for a median 5.3 years
([R01](references.md)). The primary composite of MI, stroke and cardiovascular
death was **not** reduced: 2.99% vs 3.24%, HR 0.92 (0.80-1.06).

But total myocardial infarction fell by 28% (HR 0.72, 0.59-0.90), and fatal MI by
50% (HR 0.50, 0.26-0.97). The benefit appeared after the first year. This is a
genuine secondary finding in a large, well-conducted trial — and it is a
prespecified secondary endpoint, not a data-dredged one. It is also the single
most defensible positive cardiovascular claim for ordinary fish oil.

**ASCEND** gave 15,480 adults with diabetes 1 g/day for 7.4 years and found no
reduction in serious vascular events: 8.9% vs 9.2%, RR 0.97 (0.87-1.08)
([R04](references.md)). Diabetes without established CVD is not a population that
benefits at 1 g/day.

**Cochrane's 2020 review** (86 RCTs, 162,796 participants) is the best summary
([R05](references.md)):

| Outcome | RR (95% CI) | Certainty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| All-cause mortality | 0.97 (0.93-1.01) | High |
| Cardiovascular mortality | 0.92 (0.86-0.99) | Moderate |
| Cardiovascular events | 0.96 (0.92-1.01) | High |
| CHD events | 0.91 (0.85-0.97) | Low |
| Stroke | 1.02 (0.94-1.12) | Moderate |

Read that table carefully: high-certainty **no effect** on mortality and major
events, with a small possible coronary benefit of low certainty. That is the
entire case for primary prevention in one table.

### Secondary prevention and high-dose EPA

**REDUCE-IT** is the outlier. It gave 8,179 patients with established CVD or
diabetes plus risk factors **4 g/day of icosapent ethyl** — a purified EPA ethyl
ester drug, not fish oil — and reduced the primary composite from 22.0% to 17.2%,
HR 0.75 (0.68-0.83), NNT 21 over 4.9 years ([R02](references.md)). This is a
large, consistent, clinically important result.

It is also the most contested major trial in the field. The placebo was **mineral
oil**, which may raise LDL-C and inflate the apparent benefit; the trial's
absolute risk reduction is larger than the EPA-induced triglyceride change would
predict. **STRENGTH**, testing 4 g/day of an EPA+DHA carboxylic-acid formulation
against corn oil in 13,078 high-risk patients, found **no benefit**: 12.0% vs
12.2%, HR 0.99 (0.90-1.09) ([R03](references.md)). The two trials differ in drug,
DHA content, placebo and population, and the field has not fully reconciled them.

The practical conclusion: **the REDUCE-IT result belongs to icosapent ethyl at
4 g/day under medical supervision, and cannot be transferred to fish oil
capsules.** A supplement providing 1 g/day of EPA+DHA is not "a quarter of
REDUCE-IT"; it is a different intervention at a different dose in a different
population.

### Atrial fibrillation: the real safety signal

Omega-3 raises atrial fibrillation risk dose-dependently. Gencer's meta-analysis
of 7 RCTs and 81,210 patients found HR 1.25 (1.07-1.46) overall, **1.49
(1.04-2.15) above 1 g/day**, and 1.11 per additional gram per day
([R07](references.md)). STRENGTH found AF 2.2% vs 1.3%, HR 1.69 (1.29-2.21), NNH
114 ([R03](references.md)). REDUCE-IT found 5.3% vs 3.9% ([R02](references.md)).
The EMA added a dose-dependent AF warning to omega-3-acid ethyl ester medicines
in 2023 ([R55](references.md)).

For a person with prior AF or a high AF risk, this changes the risk-benefit
calculation at doses above 1 g/day. It does not make low-dose omega-3 dangerous
for most people.

### Blood pressure, stroke, heart failure

- **Blood pressure:** a J-shaped dose-response; about **-2.6/-1.6 mmHg** at
  2-3 g/day, and **-4.5 mmHg systolic** in hypertensive adults ([R97](references.md)).
  Real, small, and not a treatment substitute.
- **Stroke:** no reduction. Cochrane RR 1.02 (0.94-1.12); the meta-analysis that
  excludes REDUCE-IT finds RR 1.05 (0.98-1.14) ([R05](references.md),
  [R08](references.md)).
- **Heart failure:** no reduction in cardiovascular mortality (OR 0.94,
  0.84-1.05) in a small evidence base ([R100](references.md)).

## Triglycerides and lipids

This is the strongest indication. At **4 g/day**, EPA+DHA or EPA alone lowers
triglycerides **20-30%** in hypertriglyceridaemia; at ≥500 mg/dL the reduction is
at least 30% ([R09](references.md)). At 1 g/day the effect is much smaller —
roughly 5-10%.

DHA raises LDL-C by about **7.2 mg/dL** versus placebo, and by **4.6 mg/dL** more
than EPA in head-to-head trials ([R61](references.md)); a controlled crossover at
2.7 g/day found DHA raised LDL 3.3% more than EPA ([R62](references.md)). EPA up
to 4 g/day has no significant LDL effect ([R45](references.md)). For a patient
with high LDL, an EPA-dominant product is the more sensible choice; for
triglycerides, either works.

## Brain and cognition

The evidence is unusually consistent for a supplement: **it does not work in
healthy people**.

- Cochrane: no effect on MMSE (MD -0.07, -0.25 to 0.10) or any cognitive domain
  in healthy older adults; no trial reported incident dementia ([R63](references.md)).
- 38 RCTs, 49,757 participants: new neurocognitive illness RR 0.98 (0.87-1.10);
  new cognitive impairment RR 0.99 (0.92-1.06); MMSE difference 0.10 points,
  statistically significant and clinically meaningless ([R64](references.md)).
- Alzheimer's disease: OmegAD found no overall benefit at 1.7 g DHA + 0.6 g EPA;
  only a 32-patient very-mild subgroup improved ([R98](references.md)). A larger
  DHA trial was also null ([R99](references.md)).

Meanwhile, **observational** studies find fish intake associated with about 20%
lower dementia risk ([R65](references.md), [R66](references.md)). The most likely
explanation is confounding: people who eat fish differ in income, education,
diet quality and health behaviours. This is the single clearest example in the
whole field of an association that does not survive randomisation.

## Mental health

- **Major depression:** pooled SMD 0.398 (0.114-0.682) across 13 RCTs, with high
  heterogeneity and low certainty ([R67](references.md)). EPA content predicts
  response; DHA dose does not ([R67](references.md), [R69](references.md)). At
  best an **adjunct** to standard treatment.
- **Perinatal depression:** one meta-analysis finds SMD 0.65 but with I²=91%
  ([R70](references.md)); a larger one (18 RCTs, 4,052 women) finds SMD -0.24 and
  advises against monotherapy ([R71](references.md)).
- **Bipolar depression:** g = 0.34, small and fragile; mania null
  ([R72](references.md)).
- **Anxiety:** g = 0.374 but low quality, and one included trial was retracted
  ([R73](references.md)).
- **ADHD:** Cochrane's 2023 review found **high-certainty no effect** on
  parent-rated symptoms (SMD -0.08) ([R74](references.md)).

## Joints, muscle and bone

- **Osteoarthritis:** pooled SMD -0.29 for pain, but the two largest and
  best-controlled trials were null — VITAL's knee substudy (1,398 people, 5.3
  years) and a 2024 krill-oil MRI trial ([R75](references.md),
  [R77](references.md), [R78](references.md)). At best a small, uncertain
  analgesic effect.
- **Rheumatoid arthritis:** older trials suggested NSAID sparing at ≥2.7 g/day;
  a certainty-rated review finds tender-joint SMD -0.20 (-0.46 to 0.05) and
  mostly low/very-low certainty, concluding that earlier positive findings may
  reflect inadequate blinding ([R79](references.md), [R80](references.md)).
- **Muscle in older adults:** +0.33 kg (0.05-0.62) in a 10-trial meta-analysis,
  an effect that disappeared when one cancer trial was excluded
  ([R82](references.md)). The largest 3-year trial found no effect on lean mass
  or incident sarcopenia ([R83](references.md)).

## Eye, pregnancy and other outcomes

- **Dry eye:** DREAM (3 g/day, 535 patients, 12 months) was null: OSDI -1.9
  (-5.0 to 1.1) ([R84](references.md)). Cochrane agrees ([R85](references.md)).
- **Macular degeneration:** AREDS2 found no reduction in progression to advanced
  AMD (HR 0.97), confirmed at 10 years ([R86](references.md), [R87](references.md)).
- **Preterm birth:** the most interesting positive signal outside lipids.
  Cochrane finds early preterm birth RR 0.58 (0.44-0.77) across 9 RCTs
  ([R88](references.md)), but the largest single trial, ORIP (5,544 women, 800 mg
  DHA), was **null** (RR 1.13) ([R91](references.md)). DOMinO found a reduction
  in a secondary outcome ([R89](references.md)). The benefit is probably real in
  some populations and dose-dependent, not universal.
- **Child cognition after prenatal DHA:** the 7-year follow-up of DOMinO found IQ
  +1.30 (-0.47 to 3.08) — null ([R90](references.md)).
- **Cancer:** VITAL found no reduction in invasive cancer (HR 1.03), and cohort
  data for colorectal cancer are null ([R01](references.md), [R101](references.md)).
- **NAFLD, CKD, cachexia, sleep:** small, inconsistent or very-low-certainty
  effects; none justifies supplementation on its own.
