# Dosage guide

The generated table with sources is
[`generated/dosing-table.md`](generated/dosing-table.md). This chapter explains
how to turn it into a practical decision.

## The dose depends entirely on the goal

| Goal | EPA+DHA per day | What to expect |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Avoid deficiency | 250 mg | Meets the EFSA adequate intake; roughly two fatty-fish meals a week |
| Primary prevention, no CVD | 0-1,000 mg | No mortality benefit; possible MI reduction at 1 g/day |
| After myocardial infarction | ~1,000 mg | Modest coronary benefit; guideline-consistent |
| Lower triglycerides | 2,000-4,000 mg | 20-30% at 4 g/day; less at 2 g |
| Severe hypertriglyceridaemia | 4,000 mg | Prescription product under medical supervision |
| Pregnancy, preterm prevention | 500-1,000 mg, ≥500 mg DHA | Discuss with obstetric care; not universal |
| Depression (adjunct) | 1,000-2,000 mg, EPA-predominant | Low-certainty adjunct only |
| Dry eye, cognition, ADHD | none justified | Trials null |

## Dose is the most common labelling trap

A 2023 survey of 255 US fish oil supplement labels found a **median combined
EPA+DHA dose of 600 mg/day**, and only **9.4% of products delivered 2,000 mg or
more** per day ([R119](references.md)). Most people taking "fish oil" are taking
a dose that has never produced a cardiovascular outcome benefit in a trial.

Read the **Supplement Facts panel**, not the front of the bottle:

```
Front:  "1,200 mg fish oil, 360 mg omega-3"
Panel:  Serving size 2 softgels
        Fish oil concentrate     2,000 mg
        EPA                        360 mg
        DHA                        240 mg
```

That bottle delivers 600 mg of EPA+DHA per day, not 1,200 mg, and not 360 mg.

## Practical protocol

**Take with a meal containing fat.** This matters most for ethyl esters, whose
absorption roughly triples with a fatty meal. It also reduces nausea and fishy
reflux.

**Split large doses.** 4 g/day is better tolerated as 2 g twice daily than as a
single dose, and splitting reduces the peak that drives burping.

**Timing within the day is irrelevant.** There is no meaningful evidence for
morning versus evening dosing.

**Choose the form by price per gram, not marketing.** rTG is modestly better
absorbed; EE is usually cheaper. If an rTG product costs 30% more per gram of
EPA+DHA, the bioavailability advantage (roughly 24% in a two-week study) does not
clearly justify it.

**Refrigerate after opening** if the label says so, keep the bottle closed, and
use liquids within their stated window. Capsules should be used by the expiry
date.

## What dose raises the omega-3 index?

The index is the best biomarker of whether supplementation is "working" at a
biochemical level. Typical Western values are 3-5%; the proposed target is
**8-11%** ([R21](references.md), [R22](references.md)). Response varies several-fold
between individuals, so dose-prediction is unreliable; if the index matters
clinically, measure it rather than guessing.

## Special populations

### Pregnancy and breastfeeding

- **Target:** about 500-1,000 mg EPA+DHA/day, including at least 500 mg DHA, from
  early pregnancy ([R88](references.md)).
- **Why:** the pooled reduction in early preterm birth is large (RR 0.58), but
  the largest single trial was null, so this is a discussion, not a universal
  prescription.
- **Avoid cod liver oil** in pregnancy: the vitamin A content is a teratogen risk
  at high intake, and the adult upper limit applies.
- **Breastfeeding:** maternal DHA intake raises milk DHA; 200-300 mg DHA/day is a
  reasonable target.

### Children

There is no established supplemental dose for healthy children, and the ADHD
evidence is a high-certainty null. DHA is required for development, but that is
an argument for adequate diet and, where indicated, formula — not for
open-ended supplementation. Discuss with a paediatrician.

### Older adults

The cognitive evidence is null. The muscle evidence is small and inconsistent.
There is no harm in 1 g/day, but it should not displace protein intake,
resistance exercise, or vitamin D where indicated.

### People with high LDL-C

Prefer **EPA-dominant** products. DHA raises LDL-C by roughly 3-7 mg/dL, and in
severe hypertriglyceridaemia this can offset part of the benefit
([R61](references.md), [R62](references.md)).

### Vegans and people with fish allergy

Algal oil is the appropriate choice. Algal DHA is bioequivalent to fish-derived
DHA, and algal EPA+DHA lowers triglycerides comparably at matched doses. Choose a
product with a declared EPA+DHA amount and a reasonable cost per gram
([R18](references.md), [R19](references.md)).

## Upper bounds and overdose

- **No tolerable upper intake level exists** for EPA or DHA; EFSA concluded the
  data are insufficient to set one ([R45](references.md)).
- **Up to 5 g/day** of EPA+DHA does not appear to increase bleeding risk and is
  the level at which the FDA exercises enforcement discretion for health claims
  ([R48](references.md)).
- **Above 5 g/day** the safety evidence is insufficient, and atrial fibrillation
  risk rises with dose.
- **Vitamin A:** the adult upper limit is 3,000 mcg RAE/day. Carlson's cod liver
  oil supplies 8.5% of it per teaspoon, typical cod liver oils about 45%, and
  high-vitamin products can exceed the limit ([R59](references.md)).
- **Vitamin D:** the adult upper limit is 4,000 IU/day (100 mcg)
  ([R58](references.md)).

There is no useful "loading phase". Omega-3 status rises over weeks to months and
plateaus; taking 10 g on day one does not accelerate the membrane incorporation
that matters.

## A decision rule

1. **Do you have a reason with trial evidence?** High triglycerides, recent MI,
   pregnancy, or a diet with almost no fish. If yes, dose for that reason.
2. **If not, is 1 g/day cheap and tolerable?** If yes, it is a reasonable
   low-risk choice with a possible small MI benefit. If it causes reflux,
   expense, or AF concern, stopping is also reasonable.
3. **Do not escalate the dose for a benefit that has not been shown** —
   cognition, joints, mood, dry eye. More grams do not buy more of an effect that
   is not there, but they do buy more atrial fibrillation risk.
