# Fish Oil and Omega-3 Supplements: An Independent Scientific Review

**A source-linked synthesis of EPA/DHA biology, clinical evidence, formulation
science, product quality and cost.**

Version 1.0.0 · 18 November 2026 · Kevin Edwards

---

## Executive summary

Omega-3 supplements are among the most heavily marketed products in the
supplement industry and among the most misunderstood. The evidence has matured
enough to support a clear verdict: **these products work for a narrow set of
indications, at specific doses, and the market charges wildly different prices
for the same nutrient.**

### What is established

| Finding | Best estimate | Certainty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 g/day EPA+DHA lowers triglycerides | **-20 to -30%** | High |
| 1 g/day reduces myocardial infarction in primary prevention | HR **0.72** (0.59-0.90) | Moderate |
| 1 g/day does not reduce the primary cardiovascular composite | HR **0.92** (0.80-1.06) | High |
| Omega-3 does not reduce all-cause mortality | RR **0.97** (0.93-1.01) | High |
| Omega-3 does not prevent dementia or cognitive decline | RR **0.98** (0.87-1.10) | Moderate |
| Omega-3 does not improve ADHD symptoms | SMD **-0.08** (-0.24 to 0.07) | High |
| Omega-3 does not improve dry eye symptoms | OSDI **-1.9** (-5.0 to 1.1) | High |
| High-dose omega-3 increases atrial fibrillation | HR **1.25** overall; **1.49** above 1 g/day | Moderate |
| Bleeding risk at usual doses is small | RR **1.09** (0.91-1.31) | High |
| Prenatal omega-3 reduces early preterm birth | RR **0.58** (0.44-0.77) | High (heterogeneous) |

### What the market does with that

- The **median US supplement delivers about 600 mg/day of EPA+DHA**
  ([R119](../docs/references.md)); the cardiovascular trials used 840-4,000 mg/day.
- **Krill oil costs $2.83-$4.59 per gram of EPA+DHA** versus **$0.30-$0.64** for
  concentrated fish oil, for the same nutrient.
- **Five of twenty** scored products do not declare enough label information to
  calculate concentration, and the best-selling mass-market format often declares
  no EPA/DHA split at all.
- Oxidation above voluntary limits is common in some markets and product
  formats — up to 50-83% of products in New Zealand and Canadian surveys — but
  uncommon in unflavoured US softgels.
- **Cod liver oil can supply 85% of the adult vitamin A upper limit in one
  teaspoon.**

### The practical verdict

Supplementation is worthwhile **if** you have high triglycerides, a recent
myocardial infarction, low fish intake, or are pregnant and have discussed it
with your clinician. It is **not** worthwhile for cognition, ADHD, dry eye,
cancer prevention, or general "heart health" in a person who already eats fish.

Buy the **cheapest high-concentration product with a lot-level freshness test**.
For most people that means a concentrated fish oil at **$0.30-$0.65 per gram of
EPA+DHA**, taken with food. Krill and algal oils are legitimate but expensive;
rTG is modestly better absorbed but rarely worth a large premium; cod liver oil
is a vitamin product with a narrow margin.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Why this review exists

Fish oil is sold with claims that range from well-supported (triglyceride
lowering) to false (krill oil is 50 times more potent) to legally actionable
(implied prevention of heart attacks or Alzheimer's disease). Consumers cannot
easily tell which is which, because:

- the front of the bottle reports **oil mass**, not EPA+DHA;
- the evidence is spread across cardiology, psychiatry, obstetrics and
  ophthalmology journals;
- quality varies by supplier, market and product format;
- certification logos are widely misunderstood.

This review addresses all four, with reproducible data.

### 1.2 Scope

Included: human randomised trials, systematic reviews, meta-analyses, large
cohorts, regulatory determinations, market quality surveys, and a 20-product
pricing and label database.

Excluded: animal and in-vitro studies as evidence of clinical benefit (used only
for mechanism), unpublished manufacturer data, and products whose labels do not
declare separate EPA and DHA.

### 1.3 How to read the numbers

Effect sizes are reported with 95% confidence intervals. "No benefit" means the
interval is compatible with no effect; it does not mean harm. Certainty is graded
High / Moderate / Low / Very low using GRADE logic. A high-certainty null result
is a strong finding; a low-certainty positive result is a weak one.

Full methods: [`docs/01-methodology.md`](../docs/01-methodology.md).

## 2. Biology: EPA and DHA are not interchangeable

EPA (20:5 n-3) and DHA (22:6 n-3) are long-chain omega-3 fatty acids. Humans
convert ALA to EPA and DHA inefficiently — a few percent at most — so dietary or
supplemental preformed EPA/DHA matters.

| | EPA | DHA |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chain / double bonds | 20:5 | 22:6 |
| Concentration in tissue | Membranes, eicosanoid precursor | Retina, brain grey matter, cardiac membranes |
| Lipid effect | Lowers TG; neutral LDL | Lowers TG; **raises LDL ~3-7 mg/dL** |
| Distinctive evidence | REDUCE-IT (as purified ethyl ester) | Infant development, retinal function |
| Typical marketing claim | "Heart health" | "Brain and vision" |

**DHA is structural.** It is the dominant omega-3 in retinal photoreceptors and
neuronal membranes, where its flexibility affects receptor signalling and
neuroprotectin D1 production. This is real physiology — and it is why DHA
*deficiency* matters. It is not evidence that supplementing a sufficient person
improves cognition, and the trials show it does not.

**EPA is the eicosanoid precursor.** It competes with arachidonic acid for
cyclooxygenase and lipoxygenase, shifting production toward resolvins and
protectins. CRP falls by about 0.4 SMD in pooled trials, but the clinical
translation is modest.

**Both lower triglycerides** by reducing hepatic VLDL secretion and increasing
fatty-acid oxidation. This is the largest and most reproducible clinical effect.

**The omega-3 index** — EPA+DHA as a percentage of red-cell fatty acids — is the
best biomarker of status. Values of **≥8%** are associated with the lowest
coronary death risk and **≤4%** with the highest; typical Western values are 3-5%
([R21](../docs/references.md), [R22](../docs/references.md)). A 1-SD higher index was associated
with HR 0.85 (0.80-0.91) for fatal CHD across 10 cohorts. That is a strong
observational association, not proof that raising the index with capsules reduces
events.

## 3. Sources and formulations

### 3.1 The four commercial sources

| Source | EPA+DHA concentration | Vitamins | Evidence base |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Fish body oil | 18-30% raw; 50-85% concentrated | None | All major outcome trials |
| Cod liver oil | 10-28% | **Vitamin A and D** | Old trials; modern use is partly a vitamin supplement |
| Krill oil | 12-19% | Astaxanthin | Small trials; no outcome data |
| Algal oil | 30-75% | None | Bioequivalence and TG trials |

### 3.2 Triglyceride, ethyl ester and rTG

Natural fish oil is triglyceride (TG). Concentration converts it to **ethyl
esters (EE)**, which can be sold as-is or re-esterified back to **rTG**.

The best-controlled comparison gave 72 volunteers ~3.3 g EPA+DHA/day for two
weeks ([R11](../docs/references.md)):

| Form | Relative bioavailability | Dose-adjusted |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Natural TG | 100% (reference) | 100% |
| Ethyl ester | **73%** | 76% |
| Free fatty acid | 91% | 86% |
| Re-esterified TG | **124%** | 134% |

Two qualifications matter more than the headline:

1. **The EE penalty is mostly a meal-fat effect.** Without fat, EE absorption was
   about 20% of the free-acid reference; with a 44 g fat meal it roughly tripled
   ([R12](../docs/references.md), [R13](../docs/references.md)). Taking EE with a meal largely
   closes the gap.
2. **This was a two-week pharmacokinetic study, not an outcomes trial.** No trial
   shows rTG produces better health outcomes than EE.

**Verdict:** rTG is probably modestly better absorbed; EE is cheaper. Pay for rTG
only if the cost per gram of EPA+DHA is competitive. "70% more absorbable" is
marketing arithmetic, not a clinical finding.

### 3.3 Krill oil

Krill oil contains phospholipid-bound EPA+DHA and astaxanthin. Both are real. The
claims built on them are not.

- In a 7-week trial, 3 g/day krill (543 mg EPA+DHA) produced similar plasma
  increases to 1.8 g/day fish oil (864 mg EPA+DHA) — krill delivered only 63% of
  the dose, so this is not superiority ([R14](../docs/references.md)).
- A 64-trial network meta-analysis found **no difference between krill and fish
  oil** for triglycerides, LDL-C or HDL-C; the effect was dose-driven
  ([R17](../docs/references.md)).
- Krill is ~12-19% EPA+DHA. A 500 mg capsule typically contains **90-95 mg** —
  one-tenth of a concentrated fish oil capsule.

**Verdict:** a legitimate source at 6-15× the cost per gram. "50× more potent" is
false.

### 3.4 Algal oil

Algal DHA is bioequivalent to DHA from cooked salmon ([R19](../docs/references.md)), and
algal EPA+DHA lowered triglycerides comparably to fish oil at matched doses
([R18](../docs/references.md)). It is the correct choice for vegans and people with fish
allergy, and it avoids wild-fishery pressure. It is also more expensive per gram
in most of the market.

### 3.5 Cod liver oil

Cod liver oil is fish oil plus the liver's vitamin stores. A leading product
supplies **370 mg EPA + 500 mg DHA per teaspoon**, plus **255 mcg RAE vitamin A**
— **85% of the adult upper limit** — and 400 IU vitamin D ([R57](../docs/references.md)).
"High-vitamin" products can be several times higher. Documented hypervitaminosis
A from cod liver oil exists ([R56](../docs/references.md)). It should be treated as a
combined vitamin A/D/omega-3 product, not a generic fish oil.

### 3.6 Free fatty acids and sn-2 claims

Free fatty acids absorb efficiently without the EE meal-fat penalty but are
rarely sold. The "sn-2 DHA" argument is biochemically real but clinically
unproven: Dyerberg found stereochemistry did not affect bioavailability, and rTG
— largely randomised across positions — was the most bioavailable form.

## 4. Cardiovascular disease

### 4.1 Primary prevention: VITAL and ASCEND

**VITAL** randomised 25,871 adults aged 50+ without CVD to 1 g/day marine omega-3
(460 mg EPA + 380 mg DHA) or olive oil for a median 5.3 years
([R01](../docs/references.md)).

| Outcome | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Major CV events (MI, stroke, CV death) | 2.99% vs 3.24%; **HR 0.92 (0.80-1.06)** |
| Total myocardial infarction | **HR 0.72 (0.59-0.90)** |
| Fatal MI | **HR 0.50 (0.26-0.97)** |
| Total stroke | HR 1.04 (0.83-1.31) |
| CV death | HR 0.96 (0.76-1.21) |
| Invasive cancer | HR 1.03 (0.93-1.13) |

The primary endpoint was null. The 28% reduction in total MI is a prespecified
secondary endpoint, emerged after year one, and is the most defensible positive
cardiovascular claim for ordinary fish oil. It is not a mortality benefit.

**ASCEND** gave 15,480 adults with diabetes 1 g/day for 7.4 years: serious
vascular events 8.9% vs 9.2%, **RR 0.97 (0.87-1.08)** ([R04](../docs/references.md)).
Diabetes without established CVD does not benefit at this dose.

### 4.2 The Cochrane synthesis

The 2020 Cochrane review pooled 86 RCTs and 162,796 participants
([R05](../docs/references.md)):

| Outcome | RR (95% CI) | Certainty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| All-cause mortality | 0.97 (0.93-1.01) | High |
| CV mortality | 0.92 (0.86-0.99) | Moderate |
| CV events | 0.96 (0.92-1.01) | High |
| CHD events | 0.91 (0.85-0.97) | Low |
| Stroke | 1.02 (0.94-1.12) | Moderate |

This is the whole case in one table: **high-certainty no effect on mortality and
major events**, with a small, low-certainty coronary signal.

### 4.3 REDUCE-IT and STRENGTH: the high-dose contradiction

**REDUCE-IT** gave 8,179 patients **4 g/day icosapent ethyl** — purified EPA
ethyl ester, not fish oil — and reduced the primary composite from 22.0% to
17.2%, **HR 0.75 (0.68-0.83)**, NNT 21 over 4.9 years ([R02](../docs/references.md)).

**STRENGTH** gave 13,078 high-risk patients **4 g/day EPA+DHA carboxylic acids**
versus corn oil and found **no benefit**: 12.0% vs 12.2%, **HR 0.99 (0.90-1.09)**
([R03](../docs/references.md)).

Why they differ is unresolved. Differences include the active agent (EPA-only
versus EPA+DHA), the formulation, the placebo (mineral oil versus corn oil), and
the populations. The mineral-oil placebo in REDUCE-IT may have raised LDL-C in
the control arm, inflating the apparent benefit.

**What follows:** the REDUCE-IT result belongs to icosapent ethyl at 4 g/day
under medical supervision. It cannot be transferred to fish oil capsules, and
"4 g of fish oil" is not the same intervention.

### 4.4 Atrial fibrillation

The clearest safety signal in the field:

| Analysis | Dose | AF result |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Gencer meta-analysis (7 RCTs, 81,210) | 0.84-4 g/day | **HR 1.25 (1.07-1.46)**; **1.49** above 1 g/day |
| Per 1 g/day increment | — | HR 1.11 (1.06-1.15) |
| STRENGTH | 4 g/day | 2.2% vs 1.3%, **HR 1.69 (1.29-2.21)**, NNH 114 |
| REDUCE-IT | 4 g/day | 5.3% vs 3.9% |
| OMEMI | 1.7 g/day | HR 1.90 (1.16-3.11) |

The EMA added a dose-dependent AF warning to omega-3-acid ethyl ester medicines
in 2023 ([R55](../docs/references.md)). At 1 g/day the absolute risk is small; at 3-4
g/day it is material.

### 4.5 Blood pressure, stroke and heart failure

- **Blood pressure:** J-shaped; about **-2.6/-1.6 mmHg** at 2-3 g/day, and
  **-4.5 mmHg systolic** in hypertensive adults ([R97](../docs/references.md)). Real but
  small.
- **Stroke:** no reduction (Cochrane RR 1.02; excluding REDUCE-IT RR 1.05)
  ([R05](../docs/references.md), [R08](../docs/references.md)).
- **Heart failure:** no reduction in CV mortality (OR 0.94, 0.84-1.05), small
  evidence base ([R100](../docs/references.md)).

## 5. Triglycerides and lipids

This is the strongest indication.

| Dose | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| 4 g/day EPA+DHA or EPA | **-20 to -30%** triglycerides |
| ≥500 mg/dL baseline | **≥30%** reduction; goal <500 to reduce pancreatitis risk |
| 1 g/day | roughly -5 to -10% |

Source: AHA 2019 science advisory ([R09](../docs/references.md)).

**The DHA/EPA distinction matters for LDL:**

- DHA vs placebo: **+7.23 mg/dL LDL** (3.98-10.5) ([R61](../docs/references.md)).
- DHA vs EPA head-to-head: **+4.63 mg/dL** (2.15-7.10) ([R61](../docs/references.md)).
- ComparED (2.7 g/day): DHA raised LDL **3.3%** more than EPA
  ([R62](../docs/references.md)).
- EPA up to 4 g/day: no significant LDL effect ([R45](../docs/references.md)).

For a patient with high LDL, an EPA-dominant product is the more sensible choice.

## 6. Brain and cognition

The evidence is unusually consistent: **omega-3 does not improve cognition in
healthy people, and does not prevent dementia.**

| Study | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| Cochrane (3 RCTs, 4,080) | MMSE **-0.07 (-0.25 to 0.10)**; no cognitive domain improved ([R63](../docs/references.md)) |
| 38 RCTs, 49,757 | New neurocognitive illness **RR 0.98 (0.87-1.10)**; MMSE **0.10 points** ([R64](../docs/references.md)) |
| OmegAD (174 AD patients) | No overall benefit at 1.7 g DHA + 0.6 g EPA; only a 32-patient very-mild subgroup ([R98](../docs/references.md)) |
| Quinn 2010 (402 AD patients) | 2 g/day DHA for 18 months: no slowing ([R99](../docs/references.md)) |

**The observational discordance.** Cohorts find fish intake associated with about
20% lower dementia risk ([R65](../docs/references.md), [R66](../docs/references.md)). The likely
explanation is confounding: people who eat fish differ in income, education, diet
and health behaviour. This is the clearest example in the field of an
association that does not survive randomisation.

**What about the brain claims?** DHA is required for normal brain and retinal
function, and the EU permits a claim that DHA "contributes to the maintenance of
normal brain function" at 250 mg/day ([R115](../docs/references.md)). That is a claim
about normal function, not about enhancement, prevention or treatment. The two
are routinely conflated.

## 7. Mental health

| Condition | Best estimate | Certainty | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Major depression | SMD **0.398** (0.114-0.682) | Low | Small adjunctive effect; EPA-predominant |
| Perinatal depression | SMD **0.65** (I²=91%) or **-0.24** in a larger review | Very low | Inconsistent; not monotherapy |
| Bipolar depression | g **0.34** (0.035-0.641) | Low | Small; mania null |
| Anxiety | g **0.374** (0.081-0.666) | Very low | One included trial retracted |
| ADHD | SMD **-0.08** (-0.24 to 0.07) | **High** | No effect |

Sources: [R67](../docs/references.md), [R69](../docs/references.md), [R70](../docs/references.md),
[R71](../docs/references.md), [R72](../docs/references.md), [R73](../docs/references.md),
[R74](../docs/references.md).

**The EPA signal** is consistent across meta-regression and dose-subgroup
analyses: formulations with a higher proportion of EPA perform better in
depression. This is a hypothesis with weak support, not an established mechanism,
and it should not be used to sell high-EPA products as antidepressants.

## 8. Joints, muscle and bone

**Osteoarthritis.** Pooled SMD for pain is **-0.29 (-0.47 to -0.11)**, a small
effect ([R75](../docs/references.md)). But the two largest, best-controlled trials were
null: VITAL's knee substudy (1,398 people, 5.3 years) found no effect on WOMAC
pain, function or stiffness ([R77](../docs/references.md)), and a 2024 krill-oil MRI
trial found no improvement in knee pain ([R78](../docs/references.md)). At best a minor
analgesic adjunct.

**Rheumatoid arthritis.** Older trials suggested NSAID sparing at ≥2.7 g/day. A
certainty-rated review of 23 studies found tender-joint SMD **-0.20 (-0.46 to
0.05)**, with mostly low or very-low certainty, and concluded that earlier
positive findings may reflect inadequate blinding ([R80](../docs/references.md)). A newer
meta-analysis reports a larger tender-joint effect (SMD -0.59) but no change in
DAS28 or CRP ([R79](../docs/references.md)).

**Muscle in older adults.** A 6-month RCT found +3.6% thigh muscle volume and
+2.3 kg grip strength at 1.86 g EPA + 1.5 g DHA/day ([R81](../docs/references.md)). A
10-trial meta-analysis found +0.33 kg muscle mass, an effect that disappeared
when one cancer trial was excluded ([R82](../docs/references.md)). The largest 3-year
trial (DO-HEALTH, 1,495 adults) found no effect on lean mass or incident
sarcopenia ([R83](../docs/references.md)). Not an established therapy.

## 9. Pregnancy and child development

**Preterm birth is the most interesting positive signal outside lipids.**

| Analysis | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| Cochrane, 9 RCTs, 5,204 women | Early preterm <34 wk: **4.6% vs 2.7%, RR 0.58 (0.44-0.77)** |
| Cochrane, 26 RCTs, 10,304 | Preterm <37 wk: **RR 0.89 (0.81-0.97)** |
| DOMinO (2,399 women, 900 mg DHA) | Early preterm **aRR 0.49 (0.25-0.94)** — secondary outcome |
| **ORIP (5,544 women, 800 mg DHA)** | Early preterm **2.2% vs 2.0%, aRR 1.13 (0.79-1.63)** — **null** |

Sources: [R88](../docs/references.md), [R89](../docs/references.md), [R91](../docs/references.md).

The pooled benefit is large; the largest single trial is null. The most plausible
explanation is that benefit depends on baseline DHA status and dose. This is a
clinical discussion, not a universal prescription.

**Child cognition.** The 7-year follow-up of DOMinO found full-scale IQ **+1.30
(-0.47 to 3.08)** — null ([R90](../docs/references.md)). A prenatal DHA attention trial
found only domain-specific, hypothesis-generating effects ([R93](../docs/references.md)).

**Other pregnancy outcomes:** preeclampsia RR 0.84 (0.69-1.01), low certainty;
gestational diabetes null; post-term delivery RR 1.61 (1.11-2.33) — a real harm
signal ([R88](../docs/references.md), [R92](../docs/references.md)).

## 11. Safety

### 11.1 Bleeding

| Evidence | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| 11 RCTs, 120,643 patients | Bleeding **RR 1.09 (0.91-1.31)** |
| High-dose purified EPA subgroup | ~50% relative, **0.6% absolute** increase |
| OPERA (cardiac surgery, 8-10 g/day pre-op) | Major bleeding **OR 0.81 (0.53-1.24)**; fewer transfusions |
| 19 trials up to 21 g/day | Clinically significant bleeding "virtually non-existent" |

Sources: [R49](../docs/references.md), [R50](../docs/references.md), [R52](../docs/references.md).

The evidence argues **against** routinely stopping omega-3 before surgery
([R51](../docs/references.md)). Patients on multiple antithrombotics should individualise
the decision.

### 11.2 Gastrointestinal

Diarrhoea OR **1.245 (1.048-1.480)**; taste disturbance OR **3.478
(1.789-6.762)** ([R60](../docs/references.md)). Prescription omega-3-acid ethyl esters:
eructation 4% vs 1% ([R116](../docs/references.md)). Omega-3 carboxylic acids: GI events
24.7% vs 14.7% in STRENGTH.

Mitigation: take with food, split the dose, use a concentrated product (fewer
capsules), or choose enteric coating (reduces burping, not absorption).

### 11.3 Atrial fibrillation

See §4.4. This is the main reason not to escalate the dose without a reason.

### 11.4 Vitamins A and D

- Adult vitamin A UL: **3,000 mcg RAE/day** ([R57](../docs/references.md),
  [R59](../docs/references.md)).
- Cod liver oil: **255 mcg RAE per teaspoon** in a leading product — 85% of the
  UL ([R57](../docs/references.md)).
- Vitamin A is teratogenic; avoid cod liver oil in pregnancy.
- Vitamin D UL: **4,000 IU/day** ([R58](../docs/references.md)).
- Hypervitaminosis A from cod liver oil is documented ([R56](../docs/references.md)).

### 11.5 Drug interactions

| Drug | Interaction | Advice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Warfarin | Case reports of INR rise; controlled trial at 3-6 g/day found no change ([R53](../docs/references.md), [R54](../docs/references.md)) | Monitor INR |
| DOACs/antiplatelets | Theoretical additive bleeding | No routine adjustment; caution with multiple agents |
| Orlistat | Reduced fat absorption | Separate dosing |
| Antihypertensives | Additive small BP reduction | Monitor |

### 11.6 Long-term and high-dose

Trials have run 5-7.4 years at 1 g/day without excess serious adverse events. At
4 g/day the main signal is AF. Above 5 g/day the safety evidence is insufficient
([R48](../docs/references.md)), and EFSA could not set a tolerable upper intake level
([R45](../docs/references.md)).

## 12. Dosage

| Goal | EPA+DHA/day | What to expect |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Avoid deficiency | 250 mg | EFSA adequate intake |
| Primary prevention | 0-1,000 mg | Possible MI reduction; no mortality benefit |
| Post-MI | ~1,000 mg | Modest coronary benefit |
| Triglycerides | 2,000-4,000 mg | 20-30% at 4 g |
| Severe hypertriglyceridaemia | 4,000 mg | Prescription, supervised |
| Pregnancy | 500-1,000 mg, ≥500 mg DHA | Discuss; preterm signal |
| Depression (adjunct) | 1,000-2,000 mg, EPA-predominant | Low certainty |
| Cognition, ADHD, dry eye | none justified | Trials null |

**The dose trap.** A 2023 survey of 255 US labels found a median **600 mg/day**
of EPA+DHA and only **9.4%** of products delivering ≥2,000 mg/day
([R119](../docs/references.md)). Most consumers are taking a dose below every
cardiovascular trial.

**Practical rules:** read the Supplement Facts panel (not the front); take with a
fat-containing meal; split doses above 2 g; timing within the day is irrelevant;
choose form by cost per gram; there is no useful loading phase.

Full guide: [`docs/05-dosage.md`](../docs/05-dosage.md).

## 13. Oxidation and rancidity

### 13.1 Limits

| Marker | Limit |
| --- | ---: |
| Peroxide value | ≤5 meq O₂/kg |
| p-Anisidine value | ≤20 |
| TOTOX = (2 × PV) + pAV | ≤26 |
| Acid value | ≤3 mg KOH/g |

These are GOED and Codex limits ([R23](../docs/references.md), [R24](../docs/references.md)).
**They are not US law.** There is no FDA oxidation limit for supplements.

Two technical caveats: p-anisidine is invalid for flavoured oils, and TOTOX is a
convenience metric with no toxicological basis ([R120](../docs/references.md)).

### 13.2 What surveys find

| Market | Sample | Finding |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| New Zealand 2014 | 32 | **50%** exceeded TOTOX; 83% exceeded PV; 3 met label |
| Canada 2013 | 171 | **50%** exceeded ≥1 limit; 39% TOTOX |
| US 2018 | 48 | 87% of unflavoured complied; mean TOTOX 19.2 |

### 13.3 Does oxidised oil harm humans?

Not at retail doses, as far as the evidence goes. A randomised trial gave healthy
volunteers **8 g/day of deliberately oxidised fish oil** (PV 18) for 3-7 weeks and
found no change in 8-iso-PGF2α, 4-HHE, 4-HNE, hs-CRP or antioxidant enzymes
([R121](../docs/references.md)). Animal harm requires extreme over-oxidation. EFSA
concluded no human limit could be derived ([R32](../docs/references.md)).

**Practical:** oxidised oil is a quality failure and may reduce the effective
dose, but it is not a proven poison. Avoid it; do not panic about it.

## 14. Contaminants

| Contaminant | EU limit (marine oils) | Reality |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mercury | 0.10 mg/kg (supplements) | Very low in refined oils (mean 0.0005 mg/kg) |
| Dioxins | 1.75 pg WHO-TEQ/g fat | Low; distillation removes 76-99% |
| Dioxins + DL-PCBs | 6.0 pg TEQ/g fat | Low; shark liver oil is an outlier |
| NDL-PCBs (ICES-6) | 200 ng/g fat | Low; shark liver oil is an outlier |

Mercury binds protein and partitions into flesh, not oil, so it is the
contaminant least likely to be a problem in a capsule. PCBs and dioxins are
lipophilic and concentrate in the oil — that is the class that matters. A UK
survey found 12 of 33 products above the then-current dioxin limit
([R34](../docs/references.md)); Japanese and Canadian studies found shark liver oil far
above fish body oils. Modern molecular distillation is the control
([R36](../docs/references.md), [R37](../docs/references.md)).

**Practical:** refined fish body oils are low-risk; shark liver and some cod liver
oils are higher-risk; distillation does not fix oxidation.

Only **IFOS** publishes lot-level oxidation data. USP and NSF are genuine quality
programmes but say nothing about efficacy. MSC and FOS certify sourcing. GOED
membership is explicitly **not** a certification.

"Pharmaceutical grade" has no legal definition. "Molecularly distilled" is a
process, not a test result.

## 16. Sourcing and sustainability

Global fish oil production is **1.0-1.3 Mt/year**, containing ~**160,000 t of
EPA+DHA** ([R105](../docs/references.md), [R106](../docs/references.md)). Allocation in 2024:

- **~64% aquaculture feed**
- **~17% direct human consumption** (supplements, food, pharma)
- **~20% other** (pet food, biofuel)

About **54%** now comes from by-products and trimmings; roughly **18 Mt** of wild
fish are still reduced each year ([R104](../docs/references.md)).

**The conversion problem:** for Norwegian salmon, the fish-in-fish-out ratio for
oil was about **1.8**, and only **~26% of feed EPA+DHA is retained in the fillet**
([R107](../docs/references.md), [R108](../docs/references.md)). Feeding wild fish oil to farmed
salmon loses most of the omega-3; direct human consumption is more efficient.

**Peru:** 10-15% of global fish oil by one estimate, ~20% by another, and a
quarter to a third of traded meal and oil by FAO. In 2026 a strong El Niño led to
the first season closing at ~25% of quota and the second being cancelled
([R112](../docs/references.md)) — a material supply shock and a climate-risk warning.

**Krill:** CCAMLR sets a 5.61 Mt precautionary limit and a 620,000 t trigger.
2025 set a record catch of 624,917 t; 2026 hit the trigger in August. Krill oil
is a small fraction of the catch (<1,000-2,700 t). The concern is localised
competition with penguins and whales, not extinction.

**Practical:** algal oil avoids fishery pressure; certified by-product oils are
better than whole-fish reduction; avoid shark liver oil; take only the dose you
need.

## 18. Regulation, recalls and misleading labels

### 18.1 Drug versus supplement

Icosapent ethyl is an FDA-approved drug (NDA 202057), ≥96% pure, at a fixed 4
g/day, with a cardiovascular indication ([R116](../docs/references.md), [R117](../docs/references.md)).
Supplements are not pre-approved, cannot make disease claims, and are not
required to demonstrate efficacy. They are not interchangeable.

### 18.2 Epanova

The prescription omega-3 carboxylic acid product failed STRENGTH (HR 0.99) and
was discontinued; approval was withdrawn at company request in 2021
([R118](../docs/references.md)). It was **not** withdrawn for safety.

### 18.3 Enforcement

FDA warning letters for omega-3 disease claims: 1st Phorm (2021, blood pressure
and triglycerides), Source-Omega (2023, Alzheimer's), plus CGMP/labelling letters
([R113](../docs/references.md), [R114](../docs/references.md)).

### 18.4 Recalls

No widely documented Class I recall of a mainstream fish oil for contamination or
potency was identified in the period reviewed. Recalls in this category are
typically for unapproved claims, CGMP violations, or allergen/labelling errors.
Claims that "fish oil is being recalled" should be checked against the FDA
database.

### 18.5 Misleading label tactics

1. "1,200 mg fish oil" with no EPA/DHA split.
2. "Total omega-3" including ALA/DPA.
3. Serving size of 2-3 softgels.
4. "Krill 50× more potent" (false).
5. "Pharmaceutical grade" (undefined).
6. "Molecularly distilled" (process, not test).
7. "Supports heart health" (structure/function, not outcomes).
8. Borrowing REDUCE-IT data for 1 g/day supplements.
9. "Mercury-free" implying all contaminants absent.
10. Certification logos without lot reports.

## 19. Limitations

1. **No product was assayed.** Quality conclusions rest on published surveys and
   certification programmes.
2. **No new meta-analysis.** Pooled estimates are reported as published; where
   reviews disagree, both are shown.
3. **Purposive sample.** Twenty products illustrate the market; medians describe
   the sample.
4. **Single price snapshot.** Prices and promotions change.
5. **Publication and funding bias.** Industry funding is common, null trials are
   less likely to be published, and several major trials used non-inert placebo
   oils.
6. **Observational confounding.** Fish-intake associations are consistently
   stronger than supplement trials; the difference is probably confounding.
7. **AI-assisted.** Checked against sources but not peer reviewed.

## 20. Conclusions

1. **Omega-3 works for triglycerides and probably for myocardial infarction.**
   It does not extend life, prevent dementia, treat ADHD, cure dry eye, or build
   meaningful muscle.
2. **Dose is the central failure of the market.** The median product delivers 600
   mg/day; the trials used 840-4,000 mg/day.
3. **Form matters less than the marketing suggests.** rTG is modestly better
   absorbed; EE is cheaper; a fatty meal narrows the gap.
4. **Krill and algal oils are real but expensive.** Krill's potency claims are
   false; algal oil is the right choice for vegans.
5. **Quality is variable but manageable.** Buy high-concentration products with
   lot-level freshness testing, avoid shark liver oil and flavoured liquids, and
   treat cod liver oil as a vitamin product.
6. **Value spans 21-fold.** The same daily dose can cost $157 or $3,348 a year.
   There is no evidence that the expensive option is better.
7. **The dose-escalation trap.** More grams buy more atrial fibrillation risk,
   not more of a benefit that has not been demonstrated.

### Practical recommendation

For a healthy adult who eats little fish: **1 g/day of a concentrated fish oil
costing under about $0.65 per gram of EPA+DHA, taken with food.** For high
triglycerides: **2-4 g/day under medical supervision.** For pregnancy: **500-1,000
mg/day including ≥500 mg DHA, after discussing it with obstetric care.** For
everyone else: **food first, and do not pay a premium for claims that the trials
do not support.**
